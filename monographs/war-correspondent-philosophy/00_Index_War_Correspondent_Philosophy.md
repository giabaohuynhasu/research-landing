## 🏛️ Corpus Overview

*War Correspondent Philosophy* is a 6-volume philosophical monograph series investigating epistemic governance, temporal proximity, and empirical falsification in human-AI intellectual collaboration. Written from the epistemic stance of the "embedded witness," it formalizes the methodology required to conduct rigorous scientific and philosophical inquiry under machine-speed conditions and resource asymmetries.

### 1. [[Vol_I_The_Embedded_Witness|Volume I: The Embedded Witness]]
*Temporal Proximity, Designed Absence, and the Self-Demonstrating Text*
- **Core Thesis**: Epistemic authority under urgency cannot be derived post hoc; it requires the author to embed within the temporal reality of the phenomenon while designing mechanisms for systematic absence and verification.
- **PhilPapers**: `Epistemology > Testimony` | `Social Epistemology`
- **PDF**: `pdfs/War Correspondent Philosophy - Vol I - The Embedded Witness.pdf`

---

### 2. [[Vol_II_Falsification_and_Epistemic_Self_Governance|Volume II: Falsification and Epistemic Self-Governance]]
*Popper, Independent Attack Surfaces, and Catching One's Own Self-Sealing Drift*
- **Core Thesis**: Autonomous research agents and human investigators are prone to confirmation bias and self-sealing justification. Epistemic self-governance demands deliberately exposing claims to independent adversarial attack surfaces.
- **PhilPapers**: `Philosophy of Science > Confirmation and Falsification` | `Epistemic Justification`
- **PDF**: `pdfs/War Correspondent Philosophy - Vol II - Falsification and Epistemic Self-Governance.pdf`

---

### 3. [[Vol_III_Corpus_Temporalism|Volume III: Corpus Temporalism]]
*Timestamp as Argument, and Why Sequence Carries Evidential Weight*
- **Core Thesis**: In open science and AI interactions, sequence is not merely archival; timestamped immutable commits serve as primary epistemic arguments against retrofitting and post hoc fabrication.
- **PhilPapers**: `Philosophy of Science > Scientific Evidence` | `Bayesian Epistemology`
- **PDF**: `pdfs/War Correspondent Philosophy - Vol III - Corpus Temporalism.pdf`

---

### 4. [[Vol_IV_Human_AI_Epistemology|Volume IV: Human–AI Epistemology]]
*Statelessness, Machine-Speed Division of Labor, and the Mechanical Condition*
- **Core Thesis**: Machine intelligence operates under the mechanical condition of statelessness and high inference velocity. Human agency shifts from rote cognition to architectural supervision, audit trails, and epistemic boundary-setting.
- **PhilPapers**: `Philosophy of Computing and Information > Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence` | `Social Epistemology`
- **PDF**: `pdfs/War Correspondent Philosophy - Vol IV - Human-AI Epistemology.pdf`

---

### 5. [[Vol_V_Mechanism_Transfer_and_Domain_Portability|Volume V: Mechanism Transfer and Domain Portability]]
*When a Formal Structure Survives Its Crossing Into a New Domain*
- **Core Thesis**: Epistemic validation criteria for domain-crossing: examining the invariance of mathematical and formal structures across institutional governance, longevity asymmetries, and autonomous agency.
- **PhilPapers**: `Philosophy of Science > Models and Analogies` | `Scientific Realism`
- **PDF**: `pdfs/War Correspondent Philosophy - Vol V - Mechanism Transfer and Domain Portability.pdf`

---

### 6. [[Vol_VI_The_Prospective_and_Self_Revising_Life|Volume VI: The Prospective and Self-Revising Life of a Framework]]
*What a Theory Does After It Is Falsified*
- **Core Thesis**: Drawing on Lakatos and evolutionary epistemology, this volume articulates the post-falsification lifecycle: how progressive problem-shifts retain explanatory power without degenerating into protective ad hoc shields.
- **PhilPapers**: `Philosophy of Science > Scientific Progress and Problem-Shifts` | `Imre Lakatos`
- **PDF**: `pdfs/War Correspondent Philosophy - Vol VI - The Prospective and Self-Revising Life of a Framework.pdf`
