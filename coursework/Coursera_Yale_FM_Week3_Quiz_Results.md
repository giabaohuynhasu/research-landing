## Lesson #8 Quiz: Debt, Bond Pricing, and Monetary Dynamics

### 1. Short-Term Interest Rates Context
- **Question:** Which of the following describes current short term interest rates?
- **Answer:** **They are approximately equal to zero** (in the historical post-GFC macroeconomic context discussed by Prof. Shiller in the baseline lectures).
- **Core Concept:** Zero lower bound (ZLB) regimes and policy rate dynamics implemented by central banks during modern liquidity management.

### 2. The Federal Funds Rate Maturity Horizon
- **Question:** What is the Federal Funds Rate and how long does it take to mature?
- **Answer:** **The shortest term interest rate in the federal government, which takes one day to mature.** (Overnight interbank borrowing rate).
- **Core Concept:** The overnight uncollateralized lending rate among depository institutions to fulfill Federal Reserve reserve requirements.

### 3. Annual Compounding Yield Mechanics
- **Question:** If you put $1,000 into an account with a 20% interest rate, how much money will you have at the end of the year if interest is compounded ONCE per year?
- **Answer:** **$1,200**
- **Calculation:** $1,000 \times (1 + 0.20)^1 = ,200.

### 4. Anatomy and Operation of Coupon Bonds
- **Question:** How do coupon bonds work?
- **Answer:** **You purchase a bond for the same price you eventually sell it for, but while it reaches maturity, you may clip "coupons" off the bond and exchange them for money.**
- **Core Concept:** Physical coupon clipping history vs. modern electronic coupon accrual generating regular periodic cash inflows until face value repayment at maturity.

### 5. Consols vs. Standard Annuities
- **Question:** What is the main difference between a consol and an annuity?
- **Answer:** **A consol pays a constant quantity (coupon) forever, whereas the annuity also pays a constant quantity but only until a fixed time T called the maturity date.**
- **Core Concept:** Consols are perpetuities ( = \frac{C}{r}$) with no fixed maturity date, historically issued by the British Treasury; annuities terminate at maturity date $.

### 6. Forward Rate Definition
- **Question:** The forward rate is:
- **Answer:** **The expected rate (yield) on a bond several months or years from now.**
- **Core Concept:** The interest rate contracted today for future borrowing/lending derived from the term structure of the yield curve.

### 7. Real Interest Rate vs. Nominal Rates (Fisher Equation)
- **Question:** The real interest rate is calculated by:
- **Answer:** **Subtracting the inflation rate from the nominal interest rate.**
- **Formula:**  \approx i - \pi$ (Exact:  + r = \frac{1+i}{1+\pi}$).

### 8. Irving Fisher's Debt Deflation Theory
- **Question:** Irving Fisher's Debt Deflation Theory starts from the observation that:
- **Answer:** **Deflation redistributed real wealth from debtors to creditors.**
- **Macro Rationale:** In a deflationary spiral, falling price levels increase the real burden of nominal debt obligations, triggering distress sales, bankruptcies, and economic contraction.

## Lesson #9 Quiz: Stocks, Equity Dilution, and Corporate Governance

### 1. Market Capitalization
- **Question:** Market capitalization is calculated by using:
- **Answer:** **The price per share and the total number of outstanding shares.**
- **Formula:** $\text{Market Cap} = P_{\text{share}} \times N_{\text{shares\_outstanding}}$.

### 2. Proportional Corporate Ownership
- **Question:** The greater an investor's ownership in a corporation is, the greater:
- **Answer:** **is the total number of shares he/she owns with respect to the total number of shares outstanding.**
- **Core Concept:** Corporate control and voting power are strictly determined by percentage ownership ($\frac{\text{shares owned}}{\text{total shares}}$), not gross share count alone.

### 3. Seniority Hierarchy in Dividend Payouts
- **Question:** A firm must make its dividend payments to __________ before it makes any dividend payments to its ___________.
- **Answer:** **preferred shareholders; common shareholders**
- **Capital Structure Rule:** Preferred equity holds contractual priority over common equity in liquidation proceeds and dividend distributions.

### 4. Basic Corporate Charter Provisions (Multi-Select)
- **Question:** The basic corporate charter:
- **Answers:**
  - **does not say that the firm ever has to raise debt. The board decides.**
  - **does not say that the firm ever has to issue warrants, convertible debt or any other debt securities.**
- **Governance Fact:** Corporate charters grant discretionary power to the Board of Directors regarding financing instruments; there is no constitutional mandate to issue debt or complex hybrid derivatives.

### 5. Myers & Majluf Pecking Order Theory
- **Question:** In the Pecking Order Theory, the companies prioritize their sources of financing in the following order:
- **Answer:** **(1) Internal financing, (2) Debt issuance, (3) Equity.**
- **Information Asymmetry Rationale:** Internal retained earnings suffer no asymmetric information discounts; debt has low information sensitivity; external equity is the most expensive due to adverse selection / signaling effects.

### 6. Equity Dilution
- **Question:** A dilution is:
- **Answer:** **A reduction in the ownership percentage of a share of stock caused by the issuance of new shares.**
- **Impact:** Existing shareholders' proportional earnings and voting weight decrease unless they exercise pre-emptive rights.

### 7. Share Repurchase Programs (Multi-Select)
- **Question:** A share repurchase is:
- **Answers:**
  - **An alternative to paying dividends in order to return cash to investors.**
  - **A program by which a company buys back its own shares from the marketplace or from its shareholders (at a fixed price).**
- **Financial Flexibility:** Buybacks provide tax-efficient capital return without establishing the rigid expectations associated with regular dividend announcements.

### 8. The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio (Multi-Select)
- **Question:** The price-to-earnings ratio:
- **Answers:**
  - **Effectively shows the number of years of earnings at which the company is valued given the current level of the share price.**
  - **Shows how much an investor is willing to pay for the stock of the company for each dollar of the company's earnings.**
- **Valuation Insight:** A multiple reflecting market growth expectations, earnings quality, and the cost of capital.

### 9. Signaling Effect of Dividend Reductions
- **Question:** Generally, a reduction in dividend is interpreted by investors as:
- **Answer:** **Bad news, with often a drop in the stock price.**
- **Signaling Theory:** Dividends are "sticky"; management rarely cuts payouts unless forced by severe persistent cash flow impairments, signaling adverse insider information.
